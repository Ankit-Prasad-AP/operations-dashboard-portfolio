What This Does

This demo shows how an operations planner can combine forecast, available inventory, open customer orders, an exact locked production week, manual planner overrides, limited-run carry, and manufacturing minimums into one four-week production recommendation. The goal is to protect committed demand first, preserve planner decisions exactly, and then use eligible future demand to stabilize weekly production.

How It Works

  1. Use the locked first week exactly; it is not rebalanced.
  2. Apply active manual planner rows exactly for the later weeks; the algorithm does not add to or change those cells.
  3. For non-manual items, calculate uncovered current-period demand after available inventory and the locked week.
  4. Carry eligible prior-period shortage forward only within a limited window. Limited-run items do not receive unrelated future forecast just to fill capacity.
  5. Use open customer orders as a coverage floor. If an order could not be protected in the exact locked week, carry it to the next week and flag the timing risk.
  6. Round non-manual production to generalized pallet and batch minimums so the recommendation is manufacturable.
  7. After committed demand is protected, pull only eligible future normal demand forward to reduce under-loaded weeks.
  8. Surface master-data reviews and planning exceptions separately so the planner can approve the tentative summary before line-level scheduling.

Portfolio Note

This public demonstration uses synthetic data and generalized operating parameters. It illustrates the planning decisions and workflow without exposing employer, customer, supplier, item, or policy details.
