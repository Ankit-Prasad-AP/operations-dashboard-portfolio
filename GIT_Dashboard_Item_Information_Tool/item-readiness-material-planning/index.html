How It Works

  1. Set the current planning period and choose a finished item.
  2. Compare normal forecast, sold-to-date volume and open production orders to understand current and immediate-next-period exposure.
  3. Keep actual scheduled production separate from proposed future production so the planner can inspect either material scenario.
  4. Explode the selected scenario through packaging and formula requirements.
  5. Apply on-hand inventory, eligible purchase orders and physical overrides to identify covered versus short materials.
  6. Review shelf-life lots, packaging transitions and historical order timing for readiness risks.
  7. Use the bill-of-materials and relationship web to understand where materials are unique or shared across finished items.
  8. Act on shortages, late supply, transition risk or production preparedness before the schedule is finalized.
